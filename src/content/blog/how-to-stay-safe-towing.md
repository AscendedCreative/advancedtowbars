Towing may seem straightforward, but doing it safely requires the right preparation, equipment, and awareness on the road. Whether you're towing a caravan, boat, or work trailer, follow these essential tips to stay safe and avoid costly mistakes.

### ✅ 1. Know Your Vehicle’s Towing Capacity

Always check your vehicle’s **towing capacity** in the owner's manual. Overloading is not only illegal but also dangerous, as it affects braking, handling, and stability. Never exceed the Gross Trailer Mass (GTM) or Aggregate Trailer Mass (ATM) limits.

### ✅ 2. Use the Right Towbar

A quality towbar matched to your vehicle is essential. Choose between **light-duty** or **heavy-duty** depending on what you’re towing. Make sure it’s installed by a certified professional and includes the correct wiring harness for your trailer lights and brakes.

### ✅ 3. Check Your Load Balance

An unbalanced load can cause swaying, reduced control, and tyre wear. Distribute weight **evenly over the axles** and aim for around 10% of the trailer’s weight to be on the towball. Use straps and tie-downs to secure cargo.

### ✅ 4. Perform a Pre-Trip Safety Check

Before every trip, inspect:

- **Tyre pressure** on both vehicle and trailer  
- **Brake lights and indicators**  
- **Safety chains and shackles**  
- **Mirrors** — use towing mirrors if needed  
- **Trailer brakes** if applicable

### ✅ 5. Drive with Extra Caution

Towing affects your **acceleration, braking, and turning**. Leave extra space between vehicles, brake earlier, and take corners wider. Avoid sudden movements, and remember that overtaking takes longer.

### ✅ 6. Follow Local Laws

Ensure you comply with all **state regulations** for towing. This includes having trailer brakes where required, weight compliance, and proper lighting.
